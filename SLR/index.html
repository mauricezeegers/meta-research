About the seminar

A free online seminar for PhD students, hosted by Maastricht University (CAPHRI and NUTRIM) with the CREST program.

Modern biomedical science faces two problems at once: far more literature than anyone can read, and declining trust in what gets published. Systematic reviews were developed to make sense of a rapidly expanding literature, but only rigorous reviews provide a reliable basis for decisions. Many published reviews fall short: assessments using AMSTAR 2 repeatedly find that most have critical methodological flaws. This seminar covers how a rigorous review is actually built, where published reviews most often go wrong, and how artificial intelligence is changing the field, for better and for worse. The second half introduces CREST, a collaborative training program in which PhD students learn evidence synthesis by carrying out a real, mentored review in their own field.

Who it is for

  • PhD students in CAPHRI and NUTRIM at Maastricht University
  • PhD students and early career researchers at other schools and universities
  • Faculty, clinician-scientists, and anyone interested in evidence synthesis, meta-research, and the responsible use of AI in research

What you will learn

By the end of the seminar, you will be able to:

  • Explain why evidence synthesis is necessary in contemporary science
  • Describe the major stages of conducting a systematic review and meta-analysis
  • Tell the main synthesis methods apart (scoping reviews, rapid reviews, overviews, network meta-analysis) and identify when each is appropriate
  • Recognize the methodological weaknesses that make most published reviews unreliable
  • Evaluate the opportunities and risks of AI-assisted evidence synthesis, including verification and reporting expectations
  • Understand what paper mills and synthetic science mean for research integrity and scientific trust
  • Identify how you could carry out a mentored systematic review through the CREST program
