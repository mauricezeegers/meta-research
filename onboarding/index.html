Welcome to Our Research Group

Welcome to our research group! We aim to provide a supportive environment where you can learn, ask questions and conduct rigorous research. This requires clear expectations, appropriate supervision and open communication.

This document sets out how we work together, manage research materials and handle data, analyses and publications. We discuss these agreements at the start of your project and add project-specific arrangements where needed. Your responsibilities will reflect your training, experience and stage of development.

1. Our Shared Values

Our research is guided by three core values:

Transparency

We document what we do, why we do it and how we reach our conclusions. We report changes to our original plans and findings that differ from our expectations.

Reproducibility

We organise data, code and documentation so that an authorised researcher can understand and reproduce our analyses.

Validity

We use methods that address the research question appropriately and draw conclusions that are supported by the study design and data.

We treat each other with respect, welcome constructive feedback and discuss mistakes and uncertainties openly. Asking for help is part of responsible research.

To familiarise yourself with our research focus and ongoing projects, visit meta-research.nl, and see meta-research.nl/gep for how we organise data and publishing.

2. Research Integrity

Our research is guided by the Netherlands Code of Conduct for Research Integrity and the UM Integrity Code of Conduct. We also follow the UM Research Data Management Code of Conduct when handling research data and software.

As part of our onboarding, you are expected to familiarise yourself with these documents, linked below.

In practice, this means that you:

  • handle data, analyses, sources and findings honestly and carefully;
  • never fabricate or falsify data or results;
  • exclude data only for scientifically justified reasons, and document these decisions transparently;
  • report findings fairly, without selectively omitting results;
  • acknowledge the work of others and avoid plagiarism;
  • disclose relevant limitations, conflicts of interest and errors;
  • distinguish between planned analyses and analyses developed during the research process.

You can discuss questions or concerns with your supervisor. If this is not appropriate or comfortable, you can contact an independent confidential adviser.

Key documents:

  • Netherlands Code of Conduct for Research Integrity (2018)
  • UM Integrity Code of Conduct
  • UM Research Data Management Code of Conduct
  • UM confidential advisers for research integrity

3. Shared Project Folder and Documentation

At the start of the project, we agree on where research materials will be stored and how they will be organised. Following our Good Epidemiological Practice (GEP), every project uses three environments with one purpose each: an institutionally approved shared project folder (the Cloud) for internal work and research data, GitHub for analysis code and reproducible output (see Section 4), and an Open Archive such as Zenodo for material that is shared publicly (see Section 8). Each file has one home (one file, one home).

Supervisors and other authorised team members receive access appropriate to their role.

We agree that:

  • project files are stored in the agreed locations;
  • files have clear names and follow an agreed folder structure;
  • a README explains how the project is organised, where materials are stored and which GitHub repository is used, if applicable;
  • important research decisions and changes are documented;
  • original data remain unchanged, and data processing is performed through documented scripts wherever possible;
  • the master dataset is the single official data source, and analyses use local copies of it.

Personal data and confidential research data must remain in the approved secure storage environment and must not be uploaded to GitHub.

Research materials must not be stored exclusively on a personal computer or in a personal account.

4. GitHub and Version Control

At the start of the project, we agree on how to use version control. Where appropriate, we use GitHub to manage analysis code, documentation and changes. Training and support will be provided when needed.

  • We identify the project repository, which is private by default, and agree on access permissions.
  • You commit and push changes regularly, with meaningful commit messages describing what has changed.
  • Each repository follows an agreed structure, with clearly named folders for code and documentation (for example R/ for shared code, docs/ for documentation and one folder per paper). A README describes the project, explains the folder structure and provides instructions for running the analyses.
  • Code includes sufficient explanation for others to understand the analytical steps.
  • Required software, packages and relevant versions are documented.
  • Research data are kept in the project folder and, as a rule, not on GitHub. An exception requires a private repository and agreement with the project lead. Personal data, confidential research data, passwords and access keys must never be uploaded to GitHub.
  • A repository is made public only after review and agreement with the project lead.
  • GitHub does not replace approved secure data storage or the formal research archive.

5. Use of Artificial Intelligence

AI tools may support activities such as programming, language editing and exploring ideas. We discuss permitted uses at the start of the project, following current institutional, educational, assessment and publication requirements.

We agree that:

  • you remain responsible for all material you use or submit;
  • you check AI-generated text, code, references and conclusions;
  • you only use code that you understand and can explain;
  • you do not enter personal data, confidential information or unpublished material into AI tools that have not been approved for that purpose;
  • you document relevant AI use, including the tool, its purpose and how you checked the output;
  • you declare AI use in every manuscript, naming the tool, its version and its purpose, following the CANGARU guidance (GEP Section 4.2).

AI use must support your learning and research. It does not replace your own understanding, scientific judgement or responsibility.

6. Communication and Supervision

At the start of the project, we agree on day-to-day supervision, meeting frequency, availability and feedback arrangements. We normally meet with students once a week, particularly during the initial stages of the project. As the project progresses, we adjust the meeting frequency together based on the student's needs and progress.

We expect you to:

  • prepare for meetings with a brief progress update, questions and relevant materials;
  • record decisions, action points and deadlines;
  • raise difficulties or potential delays promptly;
  • engage with feedback and ask for clarification when needed;
  • communicate absences and changes to your availability in good time.

You can expect your supervisors to:

  • help you develop a feasible research question and work plan;
  • provide appropriate scientific and methodological guidance;
  • provide clear, constructive feedback within agreed timeframes;
  • attend agreed meetings and discuss alternative arrangements during extended absences;
  • provide a supportive environment in which questions, mistakes and concerns can be discussed.

7. Data Use, Privacy and Security

  • You may use research data only for the agreed project and within the applicable approvals, permissions and conditions of use.
  • You receive access only to the data needed for your work.
  • Data collection or use begins only after the necessary approvals and permissions are in place.
  • You use approved storage, analysis and communication systems.
  • You do not share data with others without the required authorisation.
  • You do not attempt to identify research participants. Pseudonymised data must still be treated as personal data.
  • You immediately report any suspected data breach, loss of research data or unauthorised access to the UM ICTS Servicedesk (servicedesk-icts@maastrichtuniversity.nl; +31 43 388 5555) and inform your supervisor or project lead. Report concerns even if you are unsure whether an incident constitutes a data breach.

At the end of your project, files are transferred and access permissions and local copies are handled according to the agreed procedures.

At the start of your project, your supervisor will explain where research data must be stored, who may access them, and which retention and archiving requirements apply.

8. Research Workflow, Publication and Archiving

Your supervisor will guide you through the steps below, as applicable to your project. For projects that are already underway, we build on existing plans and approvals.

Prepare: Agree on the research question, responsibilities, timeline and intended outputs.

Plan: Review the existing research protocol and agree on the analysis plan. Your supervisor will explain which approvals, data management arrangements and registration requirements apply. The final protocol is posted on the Open Archive before data collection, and amendments are posted afterwards.

Conduct the research: Document data processing and analyses, including the reasons for changes to the original plan.

Check the work: Review key analyses, tables and conclusions with your supervisor and arrange additional checks where needed. Before submission, the first or corresponding author confirms that the data and code reproduce the reported results and runs the UM similarity check (GEP Section 4.1).

Report: Select the appropriate reporting guideline together with your supervisor, preferably early in the project. Reporting guidelines provide checklists to help you describe your research clearly and completely. Examples include STROBE for observational studies, PRISMA for systematic reviews and CONSORT for randomised trials. Use the relevant checklist when preparing your report or manuscript. Guidelines are available through the EQUATOR Network.

Prepare for publication: Discuss contributions, authorship and responsibilities with your supervisor. Before a manuscript is submitted or a preprint is posted for review, the corresponding author completes and signs the GEP checklist, saves it as PDF and sends it with each (re)submission. All authors must review and approve the final manuscript before submission. The manuscript includes the required statements (disclosures): AI use, competing interests, funding, ethical approval, acknowledgements, data and materials availability, author contributions (CRediT) and patient and public involvement.

Publish: Our aim is peer-reviewed work, not a particular journal (GEP Section 4.3). By default we first register the work publicly, for example as a preprint on medRxiv or MetaArXiv or on Zenodo, and then have it peer reviewed, usually through a journal. Post-publication review of a preprint is equally acceptable. We only publish in journals without fees or with a 100% UM APC discount, and we make work openly available through the Green Route (Taverne).

Archive and hand over: Before finishing your project, agree with your supervisor on the handover of data, code, documentation and final outputs. Ensure that files are stored in the agreed locations, that the final shareable versions are on the Open Archive, and that a README explains their contents and how to run the analyses.

We discuss authorship early and revisit it when contributions change. Authorship follows the applicable criteria and reflects actual contributions and responsibilities.

We share research outputs as openly as possible within the limits of privacy, consent, contracts and other applicable requirements. Non-significant and unexpected findings are reported with the same care as other findings.

9. Additional Requirements for PhD Candidates

Before starting your PhD project in our group, you are expected to prepare a Personal Research Plan (PRP) for discussion and approval by your supervisors and submit the completed Registration form PhD candidates (PhD-Track) to your graduate school's PhD coordinator.

You are responsible for familiarising yourself with your graduate school's PhD procedures and completing the required administrative steps on time. Once registered in TRACK, you must follow its instructions and reminders, upload the required documents and keep your information up to date.

The final PRP and Training & Supervision Plan (TSP) must be agreed with your supervisors and uploaded within the first twelve weeks of the PhD trajectory.

Forms and guidance:

  • Personal Research Plan template
  • Registration form PhD candidates (PhD-Track)
  • FHML guidance on the PRP and TSP
  • UM doctoral regulations and guidelines
